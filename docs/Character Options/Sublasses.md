#### Level 3: Adamantine Drill

When you finish a Long rest, you can use your Tinker’s Tools or Mason’s
Tools to create an adamantine tipped drill in an unoccupied space within
5 feet of yourself. This drill is large enough to dig tunnels that you
can travel through. The drill has the following traits.

Weapon Category: Simple Melee

Damage on a Hit: 1d8 Slashing plus the ability modifier used for the
attack roll

Properties: Two-handed

Mastery: Sap (you can use this property, and it doesn’t count against
the number of properties you can use with Weapon Mastery)

While holding your drill you gain the following benefits.

Enhanced Attacks. You can use your Intelligence modifier instead of your
Strength modifier for the attack and damage rolls of your Adamantine
Drill.

Adamantine Drill Bit. Whenever the Adamantine Drill hits an object, the
hit is a Critical Hit.

Spellcasting Focus. You can use the Adamantine Drill as a Spellcasting
Focus for your Artificer spells.

You can have only one drill at a time and can’t create one while you
already have one.

#### Level 3: Tunneling

While holding your drill you can use the following abilities.

Burrow. As a Magic Action, you can use your drill to tunnel under the
ground. Until the end of your next turn, you gain a Burrow speed equal
to your Speed and you gain Tremorsense with a range of 30 feet. You can
burrow through nonmagical terrain, leaving a tunnel wide enough for a
Medium sized creature to fit through.

Surface. While Burrowing, you can use your Bonus Action to rise to the
surface, if you are no more than 10 feet below the surface. You must
surface in an unoccupied space. If you surface within 5 feet of another
creature, you can make one attack against that creature as part of the
same Bonus action.

#### Level 5: Extra Attack

You can attack twice instead of once whenever you take the Attack action
on your turn. You can forgo one of your attacks when you take the Attack
action to use your Burrow ability.

#### Level 9: Dynamite

When you use your Surface ability you may cause a small explosion as you
emerge from the ground. When you do so each creature within a 10 foot
Emanation of you makes a Dexterity saving throw against your Spell Save
DC, taking 3d6 Fire damage on a failed save, or half as much on a
success. You can use this feature a number of times equal to your
Intelligence modifier (minimum of once). You regain all expended uses
when you finish a Long Rest.

Barbarian

## Path of the Dragon

Ever felt the same fury that a dragon feels when an intruder dares step
upon its horde? This fury is a daily feeling for Path of the Dragon
Barbarians. Their rage is deeply tied to the primal draconic forces. By
unleashing their fury, they unleash the power of the dragons themselves.
Flying, breathing fire, and growing scales are just a few aspects of
this transformation. These barbarians embody dragons in both body and
spirit, feeling their fury and their power.

#### Level 3: Chromatic Strike

While your Rage is active, you can deal additional damage on each of
your weapon attacks that use Strength. The extra damage equals 1d8, and
its damage type is Acid, Cold, Fire, Lightning, or Poison (choose when
you activate Rage).

#### Level 6: Furious Breath

When you activate your Rage, and as a Bonus Action on each turn while it
is active, you can unleash a breath attack in a 15-foot Cone. Each
creature in this cone must make a Dexterity saving throw (DC 8 plus your
Constitution modifier and Proficiency Bonus), taking damage equal to a
number of d6s equal to your Rage Damage bonus. On a successful save, a
creature takes half as much damage. The damage type for this feature is
the same as the one you chose for Chromatic Strike.

#### Level 10: Hardened Scales

You grow hardened scales, which enhances your Unarmored Defense. While
not wearing armor, your base AC equals 13 plus your Dexterity and
Constitution Modifiers. You can use a Shield and still gain this
benefit.

#### Level 14: Draconic Presence

When you enter your Rage you gain the following benefits.

Soar. Spectral wings sprout from your back, granting you a Fly Speed
equal to your Speed.

Roar. When you enter your Rage, you unleash a terrifying roar. Each
creature of your choice within 30 feet of you must make a Wisdom saving
throw (DC 8 plus your Constitution modifier and Proficiency Bonus). On a
failed save, a target has the Frightened condition for 1 minute (a
target can repeat the save at the end of each of its turns, ending the
effect on itself on a success).

Gore. Your Chromatic strikes now deal an additional 2d8 damage of the
chosen damage type (instead of 1d8).

Bard

## College of Law

Many bards express their art through song, dance, and even
swordsmanship. Not those that graduate from the College of Law, however.
College of Law bards use their words like a fighter would use his shield
and sword. These bards weave legal terms, magical enchantments, and
devastating arguments like a master seamstress weaves a fine silk dress.
They thrive in contract negotiations, settlement deals, and lawsuits.
Any time a conflict can be resolved with a contract and a quill is a
good day for a College of Law bard.

#### Level 3: Legal Jargon

You can lash out at foes, spouting legal jargon until their head spins.
As a Bonus Action, you can expend a use of Bardic Inspiration, rolling a
Bardic Inspiration die. When you do so, choose one creature that can
hear you within 60 feet of you. The target makes an Intelligence saving
throw against your Spell Save DC. On a failed save the target takes
Psychic damage equal to the number rolled on the Bardic Inspiration die,
and it has the Incapacitated condition until the end of its next turn.

#### Level 3: Book of Contracts

You gain a blank book, ready to be filled with all sorts of legal and
magical contracts. You can scribe a contract into the book with any
number of terms and conditions as you see fit. For each contract in your
book, a number of willing creatures equal to your Charisma modifier can
sign their name on a specific contract. When they do so, they are
magically bound by the terms of the contract and are entitled to any
benefits that it lists, that any of the other creatures bound by the
contract are able to provide. Once the contract is signed by at least
one creature, its writing can’t be changed by anything short of a
Wish spell. Any creature that violates the terms of the contract must
make a Wisdom saving throw against your Spell Save DC. On a failed save,
the target takes psychic damage equal to 5 times your Bard level and you
know its location until it remedies the violation or until it dies.

#### Level 6: Objection

You always have the spell Counterspell prepared and can cast it once
without using a spell slot. When you cast this spell, you can expend one
use of your Bardic Inspiration, rolling a Bardic Inspiration die.  When
you do so, the target has a penalty to their Constitution saving throw
equal to the number rolled on the Bardic Inspiration die.

#### Level 14: Class Action Lawsuit

When a creature you can see deals damage with an attack, you can rally
your allies to aid in their attack. Expend one use of your Bardic
Inspiration and choose a number of willing creatures equal to your
Charisma modifier (which can include yourself). Each creature can choose
to spend their Reaction to add damage to the original attack. The
original attack gains bonus Psychic damage equal to 1d12 for each
creature that spent their Reaction.

## Luck Domain

Luck always has two sides, resulting in repercussions that can help or
hurt. While fortune may not always favor them, Luck Domain clerics are
always willing to take risks to gain even the smallest benefit. They
have experienced the glorious chaos of mortal life and understand that
risks and rewards shape their fate. As a result, clerics who dedicate
their lives to the gods of luck are often gamblers, charlatans, and
performers. Driven by their own experiences with fate, these clerics use
their relationship with luck to aid those in a downward spiral and to
turn the tides on mortals that use their fortune unfavorably.

A Luck Domain cleric’s trust in their deity leads to them to be the
ultimate wild cards, as they often rely on a roll of the dice or the
flip of a coin. Whether one is on a losing or winning streak, everyone
fears the uncertainty these clerics bring to the table. Yet regardless
of their reputation, a Luck Domain cleric understands that while the
house always wins, it is their job to make sure the 'house' is on the
side of the righteous.

#### Level 3: Discard Misfortune

When a creature within 30 feet of you rolls a 1 on a D20 Test, you can
use your Reaction to allow that creature to reroll the die, and it must
use the new roll. You can use this feature a number of times equal to
your Wisdom modifier (minimum of once). You regain all expended uses
when you finish a Long Rest.

it deals additional 4d6 damage.

House Edge. Whenever the creature rolls a D20 Test, they can treat any
roll of 7 or lower as an 8 on the die.

Once you use this feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a
Long Rest. You can also restore your use of it by expending a level 6+
spell slot (no action required).

## Pestilence Domain

Clerics who pray to gods of pestilence and disease are extraordinarily
uncommon. Most clerics dedicate years to the study of purifying food,
curing diseases, and preventing death. Pestilence domain clerics, on the
other hand, believe that decay and rot are a necessary step in the
natural cycle of life. They live to spread glorious plague throughout
the land. Rotting crops, poisoning water, and spreading disease are all
necessary steps in order to cleanse the earth of the weak and for the
next cycle to begin. Sure, many will succumb to illness, but that is a
small price to pay for the sweet stench of plague ridden corpses.

#### Level 3: Leach

Once per turn, whenever you deal damage to a creature that has the
Poisoned condition with an attack or a Cleric spell, you can choose to
deal an additional 2d6 Necrotic damage. When you do so, you regain a
number of hitpoints equal to the Necrotic damage dealt by this feature.

You can use this feature a number of times equal to your Wisdom modifier
(minimum of once) and regain all expended uses when you finish a long
rest.

#### Level 3: Supernatural Decay

Constitution saving throw. On a failed save the target takes 3d8 Poison
damage and has the Poisoned condition. On a successful save, a target
takes half as much damage, isn't Poisoned, and is immune to this effect

#### Level 17: Pandemic

Whenever you cast a spell with a casting time of one Action that targets
one creature and deals Poison damage, Necrotic damage, or inflicts the
Poisoned condition, you can target another creature with that spell as
part of the same Action, given that the second target is also in the
spell’s range.

## Warrior of the Mage Slayer

Many monks use mana and magic as a byproduct of their Ki mastery. Many
go their whole lives without realizing that their Ki is even connected
to the weave at all. Mage Slayer monks are the exception. They use their
Ki to grab hold of the weave; bending it, shaping it, and blocking it.
They flow magic through their body to enhance their martial arts and Ki
capabilities. These Monks feel a very visceral connection to the weave
that few others ever experience. Magic becomes a part of them and they
become a part of it, uniting their souls with the weave. Due to their
physical prowess and unmatched control over the flow of magic, they are
often called in when a crazed sorcerer or mad wizard has amassed too
much power to be taken out by conventional means.

#### Level 3: Mage Slayer’s Mark

Once per turn when you hit a creature with a Monk weapon or an Unarmed
Strike, you can expend 1 Focus Point to mark the target you hit. The
mark lasts for 1 minute or until you use this feature to mark a second
target. The mark confers the following benefits for the duration.

        Glass Canon. The marked target must subtract 1d4 from every
Constitution saving throw it makes.

        Beat Down. Your attacks with Monk weapons and Unarmed Strikes
deal additional damage equal to your Wisdom modifier to the marked
target.

        No Hiding. You are aware of the target’s location at all times
and can see it even when it has the Invisible condition.

#### Level 6: Improved Mark

You have improved your focus and increased the capabilities of your Mage
Slayer’s Mark. Your mark now confers the following for the duration.

Weave Guide. You add 1d4 to the attack rolls you make Monk weapons or
Unarmed Strikes against targets marked by Mage Slayer’s Mark.

Sapping Speed. While marked, the target’s Speed is reduced by 10 feet.

#### Level 11: Arcane Interruption

You learn to resist the flow of magic with your Ki. You can spend 3
Focus Points to cast Counterspell  or Dispel Magic as a 3rd-level spell.
You can increase either spell’s level by 1 for each additional Focus
Point you spend, up to a number of Focus Points equal to half of your
level, rounded down. Wisdom is your spellcasting ability for these
spells.

#### Level 17: Weavebreaker

Your gaze can nullify magical effects for targets you have marked. As a
Bonus Action, you can spend 6 Focus Points to prevent a creature you
have marked with Mage Slayer’s Mark from using magic entirely.
The target must make a Wisdom saving throw or be under the following
effects for 1 minute, or until you lose your Concentration (as if you
were concentrating on a spell).

Antimagic. The target and everything it is wearing or carrying is under
the effects of an Antimagic Field.

Spellblocked. The target cannot cast spells or take the Magic Action.

Shatter Concentration. The target loses Concentration on any spell it is
currently concentrating on.

### (Too scared to delete, use for another subclass) Level 6: Arcane Flow

You can infuse arcane power into your Ki abilities. When you roll
Initiative or at the start of your turn, you can enter a state of
magical synergy. You can maintain this state for up to 1 minute. While
in this state you gain the following options.

        Eldritch Fists. When you use Flurry of Blows, you can imbue your
fists with arcane energy. You add 1d4 to the damage rolls you make as
part of Flurry of Blows.

        Retaliating Defense. When you use Patient Defense you weave

gain a +2 bonus to AC until the start of your next turn.

        Arcane Propulsion. When you use Step of the Wind, you blast off
with an arcane explosion. All creatures of your choice within 5 feet of
you must make a Constitution saving throw. On a failure, a creature
takes Force damage equal to one roll of your Martial Arts Die plus your
Wisdom modifier.

You can use this feature a number of times equal to your Wisdom Modifier
(minimum of once) , and regain all expended uses when you finish a Long
Rest.

## Field Medic

Many Rogues use their dexterity, skill, and bladework to steal, kill,
and spy. Field Medics take another path. They focus on honing their
medical practice, improving their ability to quickly apply bandages,
administer medicine, and stitch wounds. Their roguish skill makes them
excellent battlefield healers, even if they don’t have access to potent
healing magic. Field Medics are adaptable and always ready to apply an
antidote to an ally or cut a foe’s vital artery.

#### Level 3: The Doctor Is In

You gain a healer’s kit and gain Proficiency in Medicine. If you are
already proficient in medicine, you gain Expertise in Medicine. If you
already have Expertise in Medicine, you gain proficiency in a different
skill of your choice.

#### Level 3: First Aid

You can use a Bonus Action to do the following.

        Triage. Take the Help action to stabilize a creature or take the
Utilize action to use a Healer’s Kit.

        Fast Acting Medicine. You can patch up your wounds or the wounds
of another creature within 5 feet of you. The creature can roll up to
half of their Hit Dice, adding their Constitution modifier to each dice
rolled. They regain hit points equal to the total of the dice and
modifiers. A creature can only benefit from this feature once, and can
do so again after finishing a Long Rest.

#### Level 9: Improvised Antidotes

Whenever you use your Fast Acting Medicine ability, you can remove one
of the following conditions from the creature you heal: Blinded,
Deafened, Paralyzed, or Poisoned.

#### Level 13: Potent Healing

You have improved your ability to mend other’s wounds. Whenever you use
Fast Acting medicine, the creature you heal regains the maximum number
of hitpoints possible for all Hit Dice rolls.

#### Level 17: Plague Doctor

You gain the following Cunning Strike options.

Lobotomy (Cost: 4d6). You strike the through target’s skull, piercing
its brain. The target must make a Constitution saving throw. On a failed
save the target has the Stunned condition until the start of your next
turn.

Bloodletting (Cost: 2d6). You cut a vital artery or infect your target
with leeches, forcing the target to make a Dexterity saving throw. On a
failed save, the target gains 1 level of Exhaustion.

## Serpent Sorcery

Serpan sorcery is an ancient bloodline of enchantments, poisons, and
constriction. Many who possess serpent sorcery descend from medusa,
yuan-ti, or ancient snake gods. Oftentimes those who develop these
powers end up as spies, assassins, and infiltrators. Serpent sorcery may
not be the most glamorous source of mystical power, but it certainly
gets the job done. A dragon sorcerer might level a town with blasts of
fire and ice, but a serpent sorcerer will topple a kingdom with just a
few well placed toxins and enchantments.

## Pugilist

Tired of cowardly backline wizards? Wish you could just punch that orc
with a little bit more juice? Look no further than the Pugilist.
Pugilist wizards are a far cry different from the average spell chucker
and book worm. Sure they study and learn magic just like a normal wizard
would, but a pugilist believes that a sharp mind requires one to also

weaving, and brawling capabilities. After all, it takes a real wizard to

#### Level 3: Pugilist’s Armor

You can cast the Mage Armor spell on yourself without expending a spell
slot. When you cast Mage Armor in this way, you can instead set your AC
equal to 10 + your Intelligence modifier + your Dexterity modifier.

At level 14, your AC while using Mage Armor in this way increases to
13 + your Intelligence modifier + your Dexterity modifier

#### Level 3: Arcane Fist

When you deal damage with a spell that requires a melee spell attack,
you can deal additional damage equal to your Intelligence Modifier.

  
Additionally, when you cast a spell that requires a ranged spell attack,
you can choose to reduce the range of the spell to touch and make a
melee spell attack instead.

#### Level 6: Punch and Weave

Once on your turn, when you hit a creature with a melee spell attack,
you gain Temporary Hit Points equal to your Intelligence Modifier and
your movement doesn’t provoke opportunity attacks until the end of your
turn.

#### Level 10: Jab Cross

When you cast a spell with a casting time of an action, you can use your
Bonus Action to cast a Cantrip that requires a melee or ranged spell
attack. You can use this feature a number of times equal to your
Intelligence modifier and regain all expended uses when you finish a
Long Rest.

#### Level 14: Knock Out

When a creature within 5 feet of you makes an attack or casts a spell,
you can use your Reaction to expend a spell slot of level 3 or higher.
When you do so, make a melee spell attack against the creature. If the
attack lands, the target takes Force damage equal to 3d8 + your
Intelligence modifier and must make a Constitution saving throw vs your
spell save DC. On a failed save, the target has the Incapacitated
condition until the end of their turn. This attack happens before the
initiating attack or spell takes effect, interrupting the attack or
spell if the target fails its save.
