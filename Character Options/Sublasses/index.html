    Artificer

    Prospector [a](WIP)¶

    Mining engineer: include explosives, drills, and mine carts.

    Level 3: Tools of the Trade¶

    You gain the following benefits.

    Tool Proficiency. You gain proficiency with Mason’s Tools and Jeweler's Tools. If you already have one of these proficiencies, you gain proficiency with one other type of Artisan’s Tools of your choice (or with two other types if you have both).

    Explosives Crafting. When you craft an explosive using the crafting rules in the Dungeon Master’s Guide, the amount of time required to craft it is halved. (for examples of explosives, refer to my custom equipment rules, alchemist’s fire and similar throwables may also count as explosives at DM discretion)

    Level 3: Prospector Spells¶
    Artificer Level Prepared Spells
    3 Tenser’s Floating Disk, Faerie Fire[b]
    5 Shatter, Spider Climb[c]
    9 Gaseous Form, Meld into Stone
    13 Freedom of Movement, Stone Shape
    17 Passwall, Wall of Stone

    Level 3: Adamantine Drill¶

    When you finish a Long rest, you can use your Tinker’s Tools or Mason’s Tools to create an adamantine tipped drill in an unoccupied space within 5 feet of yourself. This drill is large enough to dig tunnels that you can travel through. The drill has the following traits.

    Weapon Category: Simple Melee

    Damage on a Hit: 1d8 Slashing plus the ability modifier used for the attack roll

    Properties: Two-handed

    Mastery: Sap (you can use this property, and it doesn’t count against the number of properties you can use with Weapon Mastery)

    While holding your drill you gain the following benefits.

    Enhanced Attacks. You can use your Intelligence modifier instead of your Strength modifier for the attack and damage rolls of your Adamantine Drill.

    Adamantine Drill Bit. Whenever the Adamantine Drill hits an object, the hit is a Critical Hit.

    Spellcasting Focus. You can use the Adamantine Drill as a Spellcasting Focus for your Artificer spells.

    You can have only one drill at a time and can’t create one while you already have one.

    Level 3: Tunneling¶

    While holding your drill you can use the following abilities.

    Burrow. As a Magic Action, you can use your drill to tunnel under the ground. Until the end of your next turn, you gain a Burrow speed equal to your Speed and you gain Tremorsense with a range of 30 feet. You can burrow through nonmagical terrain, leaving a tunnel wide enough for a Medium sized creature to fit through.

    Surface. While Burrowing, you can use your Bonus Action to rise to the surface, if you are no more than 10 feet below the surface. You must surface in an unoccupied space. If you surface within 5 feet of another creature, you can make one attack against that creature as part of the same Bonus action.

    Level 5: Extra Attack¶

    You can attack twice instead of once whenever you take the Attack action on your turn. You can forgo one of your attacks when you take the Attack action to use your Burrow ability.

    Level 9: Dynamite¶

    When you use your Surface ability you may cause a small explosion as you emerge from the ground. When you do so each creature within a 10 foot Emanation of you makes a Dexterity saving throw against your Spell Save DC, taking 3d6 Fire damage on a failed save, or half as much on a success. You can use this feature a number of times equal to your Intelligence modifier (minimum of once). You regain all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

    Barbarian

    Path of the Dragon¶

    Ever felt the same fury that a dragon feels when an intruder dares step upon its horde? This fury is a daily feeling for Path of the Dragon Barbarians. Their rage is deeply tied to the primal draconic forces. By unleashing their fury, they unleash the power of the dragons themselves. Flying, breathing fire, and growing scales are just a few aspects of this transformation. These barbarians embody dragons in both body and spirit, feeling their fury and their power.

    Level 3: Chromatic Strike¶

    While your Rage is active, you can deal additional damage on each of your weapon attacks that use Strength. The extra damage equals 1d8, and its damage type is Acid, Cold, Fire, Lightning, or Poison (choose when you activate Rage).

    Level 6: Furious Breath¶

    When you activate your Rage, and as a Bonus Action on each turn while it is active, you can unleash a breath attack in a 15-foot Cone. Each creature in this cone must make a Dexterity saving throw (DC 8 plus your Constitution modifier and Proficiency Bonus), taking damage equal to a number of d6s equal to your Rage Damage bonus. On a successful save, a creature takes half as much damage. The damage type for this feature is the same as the one you chose for Chromatic Strike.

    Level 10: Hardened Scales¶

    You grow hardened scales, which enhances your Unarmored Defense. While not wearing armor, your base AC equals 13 plus your Dexterity and Constitution Modifiers. You can use a Shield and still gain this benefit.

    Level 14: Draconic Presence¶

    When you enter your Rage you gain the following benefits.

    Soar. Spectral wings sprout from your back, granting you a Fly Speed equal to your Speed.

    Roar. When you enter your Rage, you unleash a terrifying roar. Each creature of your choice within 30 feet of you must make a Wisdom saving throw (DC 8 plus your Constitution modifier and Proficiency Bonus). On a failed save, a target has the Frightened condition for 1 minute (a target can repeat the save at the end of each of its turns, ending the effect on itself on a success).

    Gore. Your Chromatic strikes now deal an additional 2d8 damage of the chosen damage type (instead of 1d8).

    Bard

    College of Law¶

    Many bards express their art through song, dance, and even swordsmanship. Not those that graduate from the College of Law, however. College of Law bards use their words like a fighter would use his shield and sword. These bards weave legal terms, magical enchantments, and devastating arguments like a master seamstress weaves a fine silk dress. They thrive in contract negotiations, settlement deals, and lawsuits. Any time a conflict can be resolved with a contract and a quill is a good day for a College of Law bard.

    Level 3: Legal Jargon¶

    You can lash out at foes, spouting legal jargon until their head spins. As a Bonus Action, you can expend a use of Bardic Inspiration, rolling a Bardic Inspiration die. When you do so, choose one creature that can hear you within 60 feet of you. The target makes an Intelligence saving throw against your Spell Save DC. On a failed save the target takes Psychic damage equal to the number rolled on the Bardic Inspiration die, and it has the Incapacitated condition until the end of its next turn.

    Level 3: Book of Contracts¶

    You gain a blank book, ready to be filled with all sorts of legal and magical contracts. You can scribe a contract into the book with any number of terms and conditions as you see fit. For each contract in your book, a number of willing creatures equal to your Charisma modifier can sign their name on a specific contract. When they do so, they are magically bound by the terms of the contract and are entitled to any benefits that it lists, that any of the other creatures bound by the contract are able to provide. Once the contract is signed by at least one creature, its writing can’t be changed by anything short of a Wish spell. Any creature that violates the terms of the contract must make a Wisdom saving throw against your Spell Save DC. On a failed save, the target takes psychic damage equal to 5 times your Bard level and you know its location until it remedies the violation or until it dies.

    Level 6: Objection¶

    You always have the spell Counterspell prepared and can cast it once without using a spell slot. When you cast this spell, you can expend one use of your Bardic Inspiration, rolling a Bardic Inspiration die.  When you do so, the target has a penalty to their Constitution saving throw equal to the number rolled on the Bardic Inspiration die.

    Level 14: Class Action Lawsuit¶

    When a creature you can see deals damage with an attack, you can rally your allies to aid in their attack. Expend one use of your Bardic Inspiration and choose a number of willing creatures equal to your Charisma modifier (which can include yourself). Each creature can choose to spend their Reaction to add damage to the original attack. The original attack gains bonus Psychic damage equal to 1d12 for each creature that spent their Reaction.

    Cleric

    Luck Domain¶

    Luck always has two sides, resulting in repercussions that can help or hurt. While fortune may not always favor them, Luck Domain clerics are always willing to take risks to gain even the smallest benefit. They have experienced the glorious chaos of mortal life and understand that risks and rewards shape their fate. As a result, clerics who dedicate their lives to the gods of luck are often gamblers, charlatans, and performers. Driven by their own experiences with fate, these clerics use their relationship with luck to aid those in a downward spiral and to turn the tides on mortals that use their fortune unfavorably.

    A Luck Domain cleric’s trust in their deity leads to them to be the ultimate wild cards, as they often rely on a roll of the dice or the flip of a coin. Whether one is on a losing or winning streak, everyone fears the uncertainty these clerics bring to the table. Yet regardless of their reputation, a Luck Domain cleric understands that while the house always wins, it is their job to make sure the 'house' is on the side of the righteous.

    Level 3: Discard Misfortune¶

    When a creature within 30 feet of you rolls a 1 on a D20 Test, you can use your Reaction to allow that creature to reroll the die, and it must use the new roll. You can use this feature a number of times equal to your Wisdom modifier (minimum of once). You regain all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

    Level 3: Luck Domain Spells¶

    Your connection to this divine domain ensures you always have certain spells ready. When you reach a Cleric level specified in the Luck Domain Spells table, you thereafter always have the listed spells prepared.
    Cleric Level Prepared Spells
    3 Bane, Bless, Elminster’s Elusion, Magic Weapon
    5 Beacon of Hope, Blink
    7 Backlash, Confusion
    9 Contact Other Plane, Reincarnate

    Level 3: Roll the Dice¶

    As a Magic action, you can expend one use of your Channel Divinity to manifest a boon or bane of pure chance. Roll a d6 to determine the effect from the table below.
    1d6
    1 Snake Eye. Each creature of your choice within 30 feet of you must make a Constitution saving throw. On a failed save, a target takes 1d6 Poison damage and has the Poisoned condition until the end of its next turn.
    2 Godspeed. Each creature of your choice within 30 feet of you has their Speed increased by 10 feet for 1 minute.
    3 Bad Fortune. Each creature of your choice within 10 feet must make a Charisma saving throw or suffer the effects of the Bane spell for 1 minute.
    4 Good Fortune. Each creature of your choice within 10 feet of you gains the benefits of the Bless spell for 1 minute.
    5 Lucky Break. Each creature of your choice within 15 feet of you regains Hit Points equal to 1d6 + your Wisdom modifier.
    6 Boxcar. An ethereal box car runs along a 5 foot wide, 30 foot long line originating at a point you choose within 60 feet of you. Each creature in the area must make a Strength saving throw. On a failed save, a target takes 2d6 Force damage and falls Prone. On a successful save, the target takes half as much damage and does not fall prone.

    Level 6: Life’s a Gamble¶

    When you drop to 0 hit points, you can choose to make a single death saving throw to determine your fate. On a success, roll a number of d4s equal to your Cleric Level and regain that many Hit Points. On a failure, you suffer two death saving throw failures. Once you use this feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a Long Rest.

    Level 17: The House Always Wins¶

    As a Bonus Action, you can enter a state of heightened awareness and control for 1 minute. You see the golden threads of fate as they weave around you and you can bend them to your will. When you use this feature, you can designate any number of creatures that you can see within 30 feet of you (including yourself) to gain the following benefits.

    Beat the Odds. When the creature fails a Saving Throw, it can choose to succeed instead. A creature can only use this benefit once per use of this ability.

    Double Down. When the creature lands a Critical Hit with an Attack roll, it deals additional 4d6 damage.

    House Edge. Whenever the creature rolls a D20 Test, they can treat any roll of 7 or lower as an 8 on the die.

    Once you use this feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a Long Rest. You can also restore your use of it by expending a level 6+ spell slot (no action required).

    Pestilence Domain¶

    Clerics who pray to gods of pestilence and disease are extraordinarily uncommon. Most clerics dedicate years to the study of purifying food, curing diseases, and preventing death. Pestilence domain clerics, on the other hand, believe that decay and rot are a necessary step in the natural cycle of life. They live to spread glorious plague throughout the land. Rotting crops, poisoning water, and spreading disease are all necessary steps in order to cleanse the earth of the weak and for the next cycle to begin. Sure, many will succumb to illness, but that is a small price to pay for the sweet stench of plague ridden corpses.

    Level 3: Leach¶

    Once per turn, whenever you deal damage to a creature that has the Poisoned condition with an attack or a Cleric spell, you can choose to deal an additional 2d6 Necrotic damage. When you do so, you regain a number of hitpoints equal to the Necrotic damage dealt by this feature.

    You can use this feature a number of times equal to your Wisdom modifier (minimum of once) and regain all expended uses when you finish a long rest.

    Level 3: Pestilence Domain Spells¶

    Your connection to this divine domain ensures you always have certain spells ready. When you reach a Cleric level specified in the Pestilence Domain Spells table, you thereafter always have the listed spells prepared.
    Cleric Level Prepared Spells
    3 Inflict Wounds, Ray of Sickness, Ray of Enfeeblement, Web
    5 Stinking Cloud, Vampiric Touch
    7 Blight, Giant Insect
    9 Contagion, Insect Plague

    Level 3: Supernatural Decay¶

    As a Magic action, you present your Holy Symbol and expend a use of your Channel Divinity to emit an aura of decay in a 10-foot Emanation originating from yourself for 1 minute. Any creatures that enter the area for the first time, or start their turn in it must make a Constitution saving throw. On a failed save the target takes 3d8 Poison damage and has the Poisoned condition. On a successful save, a target takes half as much damage, isn't Poisoned, and is immune to this effect for 24 hours.

    Level 6: Improved Leach¶

    You regain all expended uses of your Leach when you finish a Short or Long Rest.

    You can now activate Leach whenever you deal damage with a melee weapon or a spell that deals Poison or Necrotic damage, even if the target doesn’t have the Poisoned condition.

    Level 17: Pandemic¶

    Whenever you cast a spell with a casting time of one Action that targets one creature and deals Poison damage, Necrotic damage, or inflicts the Poisoned condition, you can target another creature with that spell as part of the same Action, given that the second target is also in the spell’s range.

    Monk

    Warrior of the Mage Slayer¶

    Many monks use mana and magic as a byproduct of their Ki mastery. Many go their whole lives without realizing that their Ki is even connected to the weave at all. Mage Slayer monks are the exception. They use their Ki to grab hold of the weave; bending it, shaping it, and blocking it. They flow magic through their body to enhance their martial arts and Ki capabilities. These Monks feel a very visceral connection to the weave that few others ever experience. Magic becomes a part of them and they become a part of it, uniting their souls with the weave. Due to their physical prowess and unmatched control over the flow of magic, they are often called in when a crazed sorcerer or mad wizard has amassed too much power to be taken out by conventional means.

    Level 3: Mage Slayer’s Mark¶

    Once per turn when you hit a creature with a Monk weapon or an Unarmed Strike, you can expend 1 Focus Point to mark the target you hit. The mark lasts for 1 minute or until you use this feature to mark a second target. The mark confers the following benefits for the duration.

    Glass Canon. The marked target must subtract 1d4 from every Constitution saving throw it makes.

    Beat Down. Your attacks with Monk weapons and Unarmed Strikes deal additional damage equal to your Wisdom modifier to the marked target.

    No Hiding. You are aware of the target’s location at all times and can see it even when it has the Invisible condition.

    Level 6: Improved Mark¶

    You have improved your focus and increased the capabilities of your Mage Slayer’s Mark. Your mark now confers the following for the duration.

    Weave Guide. You add 1d4 to the attack rolls you make Monk weapons or Unarmed Strikes against targets marked by Mage Slayer’s Mark.

    Sapping Speed. While marked, the target’s Speed is reduced by 10 feet.

    Level 11: Arcane Interruption¶

    You learn to resist the flow of magic with your Ki. You can spend 3 Focus Points to cast Counterspell  or Dispel Magic as a 3rd-level spell. You can increase either spell’s level by 1 for each additional Focus Point you spend, up to a number of Focus Points equal to half of your level, rounded down. Wisdom is your spellcasting ability for these spells.

    Level 17: Weavebreaker¶

    Your gaze can nullify magical effects for targets you have marked. As a Bonus Action, you can spend 6 Focus Points to prevent a creature you have marked with Mage Slayer’s Mark from using magic entirely. The target must make a Wisdom saving throw or be under the following effects for 1 minute, or until you lose your Concentration (as if you were concentrating on a spell).

    Antimagic. The target and everything it is wearing or carrying is under the effects of an Antimagic Field.

    Spellblocked. The target cannot cast spells or take the Magic Action.

    Shatter Concentration. The target loses Concentration on any spell it is currently concentrating on.

    (Too scared to delete, use for another subclass) Level 6: Arcane Flow¶

    You can infuse arcane power into your Ki abilities. When you roll Initiative or at the start of your turn, you can enter a state of magical synergy. You can maintain this state for up to 1 minute. While in this state you gain the following options.

    Eldritch Fists. When you use Flurry of Blows, you can imbue your fists with arcane energy. You add 1d4 to the damage rolls you make as part of Flurry of Blows.

    Retaliating Defense. When you use Patient Defense you weave magic to further protect yourself. After you use Patient Defense you gain a +2 bonus to AC until the start of your next turn.

    Arcane Propulsion. When you use Step of the Wind, you blast off with an arcane explosion. All creatures of your choice within 5 feet of you must make a Constitution saving throw. On a failure, a creature takes Force damage equal to one roll of your Martial Arts Die plus your Wisdom modifier.

    You can use this feature a number of times equal to your Wisdom Modifier (minimum of once) , and regain all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

    Paladin

    Oath of the Sunlight Guardian¶

    Paladins that take upon the Oath of the Sunlight Guardian swear to protect the innocent and bring light to the darkness. These paladins have a deep connection to gods of light, sunshine, and protection. Often, sunlight guardians will favor tactics that save their allies over damaging their foes. While burning the evil with holy light is a necessary job of any paladin, Sunlight Guardians tend to favor shielding the innocent than punishing the wicked.

    Level 3: Oath of the Sunlight Guardian Spells¶
    Paladin Level Prepared Spells
    3 Faerie Fire, Sanctuary
    5 Shining Smite, Warding Bond
    9 Blinding Smite, Daylight
    13 Fire Shield, Guardian of Faith
    17 Dispel Evil and Good, Summon Celestial

    Level 3: Radiant Shell¶

    As a Magic action on your turn, you can expend one use of your Channel Divinity to shed Bright Light in a 10-foot Emanation around you for 1 minute. Each creature of your choice in the area gains a bonus to AC equal to half your Proficiency Bonus (rounded down).

    Level 7: Sunlight Sacrifice¶

    Once per turn, when you deal Radiant damage to a creature with a weapon attack or a Spell, you can forgo dealing any Radiant damage, instead granting Temporary hit points to each creature within your aura of protection (including yourself). The number of Temporary hit points is equal to the amount of Radiant damage you would have dealt.

    Level 15: Solar Protection¶

    When a creature within your Aura of Protection is hit with an attack roll, you can take a Reaction to halve the attack's damage against the creature.

    Level 20: Sunbringer¶

    As a Bonus Action, you can embody the sun itself for 1 minute, empowering your Aura of Protection. Once you use this feature, you can’t use it again until you finish a Long Rest. You can also restore your use of it by expending a level 5 spell slot (no action required).

    Protector. Attacks against creatures of your choice in the aura have Disadvantage.

    Radiance. Creatures of your choice in the aura can deal an extra 1d8 Radiant damage on attacks with weapons and Unarmed Strikes.

    Sunlight. The aura is filled with Bright Light that is Sunlight.

    Rogue

    Field Medic¶

    Many Rogues use their dexterity, skill, and bladework to steal, kill, and spy. Field Medics take another path. They focus on honing their medical practice, improving their ability to quickly apply bandages, administer medicine, and stitch wounds. Their roguish skill makes them excellent battlefield healers, even if they don’t have access to potent healing magic. Field Medics are adaptable and always ready to apply an antidote to an ally or cut a foe’s vital artery.

    Level 3: The Doctor Is In¶

    You gain a healer’s kit and gain Proficiency in Medicine. If you are already proficient in medicine, you gain Expertise in Medicine. If you already have Expertise in Medicine, you gain proficiency in a different skill of your choice.

    Level 3: First Aid¶

    You can use a Bonus Action to do the following.

    Triage. Take the Help action to stabilize a creature or take the Utilize action to use a Healer’s Kit.

    Fast Acting Medicine. You can patch up your wounds or the wounds of another creature within 5 feet of you. The creature can roll up to half of their Hit Dice, adding their Constitution modifier to each dice rolled. They regain hit points equal to the total of the dice and modifiers. A creature can only benefit from this feature once, and can do so again after finishing a Long Rest.

    Level 9: Improvised Antidotes¶

    Whenever you use your Fast Acting Medicine ability, you can remove one of the following conditions from the creature you heal: Blinded, Deafened, Paralyzed, or Poisoned.

    Level 13: Potent Healing¶

    You have improved your ability to mend other’s wounds. Whenever you use Fast Acting medicine, the creature you heal regains the maximum number of hitpoints possible for all Hit Dice rolls.

    Level 17: Plague Doctor¶

    You gain the following Cunning Strike options.

    Lobotomy (Cost: 4d6). You strike the through target’s skull, piercing its brain. The target must make a Constitution saving throw. On a failed save the target has the Stunned condition until the start of your next turn.

    Bloodletting (Cost: 2d6). You cut a vital artery or infect your target with leeches, forcing the target to make a Dexterity saving throw. On a failed save, the target gains 1 level of Exhaustion.

    Sorcerer

    Serpent Sorcery¶

    Serpan sorcery is an ancient bloodline of enchantments, poisons, and constriction. Many who possess serpent sorcery descend from medusa, yuan-ti, or ancient snake gods. Oftentimes those who develop these powers end up as spies, assassins, and infiltrators. Serpent sorcery may not be the most glamorous source of mystical power, but it certainly gets the job done. A dragon sorcerer might level a town with blasts of fire and ice, but a serpent sorcerer will topple a kingdom with just a few well placed toxins and enchantments.

    Level 3: Serpent Spells¶

    When you reach a Sorcerer level specified in the Serpent Spells table, you thereafter always have the listed spells prepared.
    Sorcerer Level Prepared Spells
    3 Charm Person, Ray of Sickness, Suggestion, Summon Beast
    5 Syluné’s Viper, Hypnotic Pattern
    7 Charm Monster, Grasping Vine
    9 Cloudkill, Geas

    Level 3: Snake Charm¶

    When you charm a creature with a level 1+ spell, that charm can’t be broken as a result of you dealing Poison damage to that creature.

    Level 6: Venomous¶

    Whenever you Poison damage with a spell, you can add your Charisma modifier to one damage roll of that spell. Additionally, you have resistance to Poison damage and have Advantage on saving throws made to avoid or end the Poisoned condition.

    Level 14: Serpent Form¶

    You can cast Polymorph without a spell slot, targeting yourself and selecting one of the following options: Giant Constrictor Snake, Giant Venomous Snake, or Flying Snake. You can use this feature twice and regain the ability to do so when you finish a Short or Long Rest.

    Level 18: Medusa¶

    You can cast Flesh to Stone without using a spell slot. When you do so, the target has Disadvantage on saves made to avoid the spell’s effects. Once you use this feature you can’t do so again until you finish a Long Rest. You can also restore your use of it by expending a level 6 spell slot (no action required).

    Warlock

    Trickster Patron¶

    Level 3: Belittle¶

    When you cast Vicious Mockery, if the target misses the next attack it makes before the end of its next turn, you can use your Reaction to cast Vicious Mockery again at the creature.

    Level 3: Trickster Spells¶
    Warlock Level Spells
    3 Charm Person, Invisibility, Phantasmal Force, Tasha’s Hideous Laughter, Vicious Mockery
    5 Hypnotic Pattern, Major Image
    7 Greater Invisibility, Confusion
    9 Dream, Modify Memory

    Level 6: Trickster’s Escape¶

    When you are hit by an attack, you can take a Reaction to teleport to an unoccupied space within 30 feet of you, and you gain the Invisible condition for 1 minute, or until you attack or cast a spell. You can use this feature once and regain the ability to do so when you finish a Short Rest.

    Level 10: Convincing Illusions¶

    When a creature takes the Study action to determine if an illusion that you created is real, they have Disadvantage on their Intelligence (Investigation) check. Additionally, while a creature perceives one of your illusions, you gain Advantage on Charisma checks against that creature.

    Level 14: Subtlety¶

    When you cast a spell while you have the Invisible condition, targets of that spell have Disadvantage on any saving throws made to avoid the spell.

    Wizard

    Pugilist¶

    Tired of cowardly backline wizards? Wish you could just punch that orc with a little bit more juice? Look no further than the Pugilist. Pugilist wizards are a far cry different from the average spell chucker and book worm. Sure they study and learn magic just like a normal wizard would, but a pugilist believes that a sharp mind requires one to also sharpen the body. Pugilists use their magic to enhance their punching, weaving, and brawling capabilities. After all, it takes a real wizard to test their magical abilities head on against a stronger opponent.

    Level 3: Pugilist’s Armor¶

    You can cast the Mage Armor spell on yourself without expending a spell slot. When you cast Mage Armor in this way, you can instead set your AC equal to 10 + your Intelligence modifier + your Dexterity modifier.

    At level 14, your AC while using Mage Armor in this way increases to 13 + your Intelligence modifier + your Dexterity modifier

    Level 3: Arcane Fist¶

    When you deal damage with a spell that requires a melee spell attack, you can deal additional damage equal to your Intelligence Modifier.

    Additionally, when you cast a spell that requires a ranged spell attack, you can choose to reduce the range of the spell to touch and make a melee spell attack instead.

    Level 6: Punch and Weave¶

    Once on your turn, when you hit a creature with a melee spell attack, you gain Temporary Hit Points equal to your Intelligence Modifier and your movement doesn’t provoke opportunity attacks until the end of your turn.

    Level 10: Jab Cross¶

    When you cast a spell with a casting time of an action, you can use your Bonus Action to cast a Cantrip that requires a melee or ranged spell attack. You can use this feature a number of times equal to your Intelligence modifier and regain all expended uses when you finish a Long Rest.

    Level 14: Knock Out¶

    When a creature within 5 feet of you makes an attack or casts a spell, you can use your Reaction to expend a spell slot of level 3 or higher. When you do so, make a melee spell attack against the creature. If the attack lands, the target takes Force damage equal to 3d8 + your Intelligence modifier and must make a Constitution saving throw vs your spell save DC. On a failed save, the target has the Incapacitated condition until the end of their turn. This attack happens before the initiating attack or spell takes effect, interrupting the attack or spell if the target fails its save.
